# AFS technical assessment

We want to investigate water content in a petroleum line using MIR ATR. The
analyzer would be in another building. We already have a silica fiber cable
from a visible or NIR measurement setup and would like to reuse it. The link
length, routing and operating conditions have not yet been supplied. Can the
assistant approve this concept?

Evidence release: afs-official-v2

Source citations: present_with_gaps (not scientific verification)

Decision status: Further clarification needed

## Answer

The concept cannot be approved on the present evidence; it can only be assessed provisionally. The supplied water-in-petroleum application shows absorbance spectra for pure oil and 0.1 to 2.0 percent water over 4000 to 2000 cm-1 (2.5 to 5 µm), using ATR probes with PEEK shafts [1]. The reused silica cable is listed with a nominal transmission range of 350 to 2400 nm, which ends before that band [2]. The 100 to 300 m figure applies only to VIS-NIR silica cables and gives no length for mid-IR fibers [3].

Mid-IR ATR fiber probes are described for liquid samples using high-transmission mid-IR fiber optics [4]. Immersion ATR probes are described as allowing analysis at a significant distance from the spectrometer [5]. Nominal mid-IR fiber families include chalcogenide 1.1 to 6.5 µm and polycrystalline 3 to 17 µm [2]. On this basis a remote mid-IR ATR concept is plausible only with a mid-IR transmitting fiber link, and the link length and loss remain unestablished.

No link length, routing, fiber specification, process temperature, pressure or cleaning data have been supplied, so the remote link cannot be assessed. The open question is therefore unresolved rather than failed.

First obtain the existing silica cable's fiber specification together with the intended link length, routing and process conditions. If the cable's mid-IR transmission cannot be confirmed by its specification, plan a mid-IR transmitting fiber link and confirm it with the fiber supplier.

Then run laboratory screening with petroleum samples at several reference water levels, measured with a mid-IR ATR probe through a short reference fiber, through the planned link length or a representative loop, and, as an empirical check of the 2.5 to 5 µm band, through the existing silica cable. Use an independent reference method agreed with the customer, such as Karl Fischer titration, and validate calibration on held-out batches.

## Approaches worth testing

### Mid-IR ATR probe with mid-IR transmitting fiber link to remote analyzer

A supplied slide shows absorbance spectra for pure oil and 0.1 to 2.0 percent water in petroleum over 4000 to 2000 cm-1 (2.5 to 5 µm). [1]

The same slide does not state the spectrometer, fiber type or length, probe temperature or pressure ratings, calibration model, detection limit or quantification band. [1]

Mid-IR ATR fiber probes are described for liquid and soft samples using high-transmission mid-IR fiber optics. [4]

Immersion ATR probes are described as allowing analysis at a significant distance from the spectrometer without sampling. [5]

Nominal transmission ranges are 1.1 to 6.5 µm for chalcogenide As-S fibers and 3 to 17 µm for polycrystalline fibers. [2]

Strong mid-IR absorption limits transmission measurement to very thin samples, which is why ATR attachments and probes are used. [6]

The method table rates IR spectroscopy as ATR probe with high cost, high selectivity and average sensitivity; these are qualitative ratings, not measured performance. [7, 8]

**Measurement / probe concept:** Mid-IR ATR tip at the petroleum line, connected by a mid-IR transmitting fiber to an analyzer in another building. The source ATR probe with PEEK shaft is a labelled example only; installation depends on confirmed process compatibility and link specification. [1, 4, 5, 2, 6, 7, 8]

**Spectral range:** The water bands in the cited slide span 4000 to 2000 cm-1 (2.5 to 5 µm). Nominal fiber transmission ranges (chalcogenide 1.1 to 6.5 µm, polycrystalline 3 to 17 µm) are fiber properties, not confirmed system coverage. [1, 4, 5, 2, 6, 7, 8]

**Limitations:**

- No inspected source gives link loss or maximum mid-IR link length for this configuration.
- The existing silica VIS-NIR cable cannot be assumed to transmit the 2.5 to 5 µm band.

**Assumptions to confirm:**

- A mid-IR transmitting fiber link has acceptable loss at the unknown link length.
- The ATR tip and probe materials are compatible with the petroleum line's temperature, pressure and cleaning conditions.
- Calibration against an agreed reference method can be established across the customer's water range and process variation.

## Important clarification questions

- What are the link length, routing, the existing silica cable's fiber specification, and the process temperature, pressure and cleaning or access conditions at the petroleum line? — Whether a mid-IR transmitting fiber link is needed, whether link loss is adequate, and whether the probe interface can be installed and cleaned.
- What water-content range, acceptable error against the reference method, and required response time must the measurement meet? — The validation design and whether the candidate meets the stated requirement.
- Which petroleum products and water-content levels must be covered by the calibration and validation? — Sample selection, calibration scope and whether one calibration is sufficient across process variation.

## Shared risks and evidence gaps

- The existing silica VIS-NIR cable's nominal transmission range ends at 2400 nm, below the 2.5 to 5 µm band of the water application, so its reuse for this mid-IR measurement is not supported by the inspected evidence. [2, 1]
- The source ATR probe used in microwave furnaces is company material and is not technical validation for this petroleum line.

## First feasibility study

Decide whether a mid-IR ATR probe with a mid-IR transmitting fiber link to the remote analyzer meets the customer-agreed water-content error and response requirements, and whether the existing silica cable can transmit usable spectra in the 2.5 to 5 µm band.

1. Gather the fiber specification, planned link length, routing and process conditions before building the test setup.
2. Prepare petroleum samples covering the customer-agreed water range, including pure oil, and measure reference water content with an independent method agreed by the customer, such as Karl Fischer titration.
3. Measure with the mid-IR ATR probe through a short reference fiber, through the planned link length or a representative loop, and through the existing silica cable as an empirical check of the 2.5 to 5 µm band.
4. Repeat measurements across temperatures and batches representing realistic process variation.
5. Fit calibration on training batches and validate on independent held-out batches; do not use reference measurements alone as validation.

## Criteria to agree and check

- Whether error against the reference method at the planned link length meets the customer-agreed limit.
- Whether spectra through the existing silica cable are usable in the 2.5 to 5 µm band; if not, the reuse concept is not adequate.
- Whether a shorter or simpler configuration performs adequately if the remote link is not needed.
- Whether results are inconclusive or no adequate candidate is found; these are valid outcomes.
- Statistical significance alone does not establish practical usefulness; compare against the customer's requirements.
