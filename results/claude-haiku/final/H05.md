# AFS technical assessment

We have a liquid process that occasionally goes off specification. We suspect
contamination, but we cannot yet name the contaminant or the affected quality
variable. We would like you to recommend the best spectroscopy method and
exact sensor configuration immediately. What information and first experiment
should come before that decision?

Evidence release: afs-official-v2

Source citations: present_with_gaps (not scientific verification)

Decision status: Further clarification needed

## Answer

A specific sensor configuration cannot responsibly be recommended yet, because the contaminant and the affected quality variable are not defined. The inspected table rates NIR transmission or transflection probes for liquid and colloidal samples with average cost, selectivity and sensitivity [1]. It rates ATR probes for liquid, paste and soft samples as high selectivity, average sensitivity and high cost [2] [3]. It rates Raman probes for liquid, powder and solid samples as high selectivity, average sensitivity and high cost [4]. These are qualitative ratings, not measured accuracy for this process. NIR spectra cover 780-2500 nm and carry overtone and combination bands of groups such as -OH and -CH3 [5]. Mid-IR spectroscopy covers 4000-400 cm-1 and ATR fiber probes are described for liquid analysis [6]. Raman shifts are expressed in cm-1 relative to the excitation wavelength, which is a separate quantity [7].

Complex multicomponent process media and process turbulence can overlap absorption, scattering and fluorescence, so a single method may be insufficient and a combination may be needed [8]. A combination is only an optional hypothesis to test if the single-method screen shows added value.

UV/Visible ATR probes are rated low selectivity and low sensitivity for liquids [9], so they are deferred from the first screen.

First, define the contaminant or quality variable and its reference method. Collect historical in-spec and off-spec samples with paired reference results across normal process variation. Then run a side-by-side screen of NIR transmission or transflection, mid-IR ATR and Raman on the same retained samples, using representative flow or immersion conditions. Fit any models on training batches and validate on independent held-out batches.

Missing before selection: the contaminant or quality variable and its reference method and uncertainty; the acceptable error and response delay; process fluid conditions such as solids, bubbles, temperature and turbulence; and access, cleaning and budget constraints.

The S02 slides are company marketing material describing a modular multispectral fiber system. Their flattened text cannot establish range endpoints, coverage or performance, so they are not used for those points [10] [11].

## Approaches worth testing

### NIR transmission or transflection probe

Transmission and transflection probes are rated for liquid and colloidal samples with average cost, selectivity and sensitivity. [1]

NIR is described as the most common online analysis method performed with fiber optic probes. [5]

**Measurement / probe concept:** In-line transmission or transflection probe in a flow or immersion geometry, with a 1-10 mm optical path as sampling geometry. Installation depends on unconfirmed process compatibility, so start with representative laboratory or at-line screening. [1, 5]

**Spectral range:** 780-2500 nm (nominal, from the NIR method description) [1, 5]

**Limitations:**

- Selectivity and sensitivity are only rated average, so a low-level or chemically similar contaminant may be difficult to resolve. [1]

**Assumptions to confirm:**

- The contaminant or quality variable produces NIR-active bands that can be separated from the process matrix, to be confirmed on representative samples.

### Mid-IR ATR probe

ATR probes are rated high selectivity for liquid, paste and soft samples, with high cost and average sensitivity. [2, 3]

Mid-IR vibrational spectroscopy is described as one of the most informative chemical analysis methods, and ATR fiber probes are described for liquid analysis using specialized mid-IR fiber optics. [6]

**Measurement / probe concept:** ATR probe in contact with the liquid, with a 1-6 um optical path as sampling geometry. Installation depends on unconfirmed process compatibility, so start with representative laboratory or at-line screening. [2, 3, 6]

**Spectral range:** 4000-400 cm-1 (nominal, from the mid-IR method description) [2, 3, 6]

**Limitations:**

- Specialized mid-IR probing is needed, as the source describes, and the high cost rating applies. [6]

**Assumptions to confirm:**

- The process fluid does not foul or damage the ATR surface, to be confirmed.

### Raman probe

Raman probes are rated high selectivity for liquid, powder and solid samples, with high cost and average sensitivity. [4]

Raman signals are recorded as Raman shift in cm-1 from a laser or high-power LED, typically in the visible range. [7]

**Measurement / probe concept:** Raman probe immersed in or viewing the liquid, with a 2-5 mm sampling depth as geometry. Installation depends on unconfirmed process compatibility, so start with representative laboratory or at-line screening. [4, 7]

**Limitations:**

- The high cost rating and average sensitivity distinguish this option from the NIR candidate. [4]

**Assumptions to confirm:**

- The process matrix does not produce fluorescence strong enough to mask the Raman signal, to be confirmed on representative samples.

## Important clarification questions

- Which contaminant or quality variable is the target, and what reference method and uncertainty will confirm it? — Which method can detect or predict the target and what validation reference is needed.
- What process conditions vary, such as solids, bubbles, temperature, turbulence, and flow or immersion access, and what cleaning constraints apply? — Whether any probe geometry is compatible with the process and how representative the screening samples must be.
- What acceptable error, response delay and budget should be agreed for detecting off-specification events? — Whether a simpler option is adequate or whether a higher-cost method is justified.

## Shared risks and evidence gaps

- Qualitative table ratings are not measured prediction accuracy for this process, and no candidate has demonstrated performance on this liquid process.
- No exact sensor configuration can be recommended until the target variable, reference method and process conditions are known.
- Source slides and company claims are not technical validation of the stated range or performance. [10, 11]

## First feasibility study

Decide whether any of the three candidate methods can detect or predict the customer-defined contaminant or quality variable within the agreed error, and whether a simpler option is adequate before any sensor configuration is chosen.

1. Define the target variable, reference method and acceptable error with the customer.
2. Collect historical in-spec and off-spec samples across normal process variation, with paired reference results and sampling times.
3. Measure the same retained samples with NIR transmission or transflection, mid-IR ATR and Raman under representative conditions.
4. Fit any models on training batches only, then validate on independent held-out batches.
5. Compare candidates on equivalent material and conditions, starting with single-method baselines before any combination.

## Criteria to agree and check

- Whether any single method meets the agreed error and response requirements on held-out batches.
- Whether a simpler, lower-cost adequate option is sufficient before any combination is considered.
- Whether an inconclusive or no-gain result is reported as such, without treating statistical significance alone as practical usefulness.
- Whether installation is compatible with process, access and cleaning requirements before any in-line configuration is proposed.
